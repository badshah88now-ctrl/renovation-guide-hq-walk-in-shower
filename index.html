Walk-In Shower Cost in 2026

A walk-in shower typically costs $4,000–$12,000 in 2026. Angi's 2026 data puts the average shower installation at $8,204, with most homeowners spending $4,448–$12,373, and lists walk-in showers specifically at $1,150–$8,000. A separate survey of homeowners found a wider $4,000–$20,000 spread averaging about $12,000, while a bathroom-industry guide citing Angi 2026 figures lands at $6,000–$12,000 with a $9,000 average. The sources disagree because they measure different things — a prefab kit, a tiled walk-in, and a curbless custom build are three different projects wearing one name.

A basic prefab kit installed runs 2–3 days. A custom tiled walk-in takes 3–5 days of active work, and a complex curbless build can run 1–3 weeks. Budget tiers tell the same story in dollars: a refresh tier at $3,000–$5,000, a midrange tier at $8,000–$12,000, and a premium tier at $15,000–$25,000 or more.

Seven factors move a quote most. First, shower size — every extra square foot is tile, waterproofing, and labor. Second, tile versus prefab walls, the single biggest fork in the road. Third, glass: framed versus frameless, with frameless costing more for thicker glass and heavier hardware. Fourth, plumbing: keeping the drain and supplies in the same spot versus relocating them. Fifth, the waterproofing system, which is invisible once tiled and expensive to fix later. Sixth, labor share. Seventh, hidden conditions — what the demolition finds behind the old walls.

The most common expensive mistake is treating waterproofing as an afterthought. A walk-in shower has no curb to contain a leak, so the waterproofing layer is the shower. Ask any bidder exactly which system they use, whether the pan and walls are flood-tested before tile goes on, and what warranty covers the waterproofing itself. A contractor who cannot answer that clearly is not the right contractor for a curbless build.
